OPEN ASSUMPTIONS

How this prototype works

From evidence to probability

Each forecast estimates a Democratic minus Republican vote margin. A weak neutral normal prior is updated with either a published polling average or raw polls, never both when they overlap.

Polls decay with a 21-day half-life. Each pollster’s total precision is capped at its strongest observation. Forecast variance includes sampling uncertainty, a pollster allowance, national error, state error, local error and time remaining.

The win estimate is the normal probability of a margin above zero. The displayed 80% interval describes possible vote margins, not a confidence interval on the win probability.

Rating baselines for unpolled races

Imported Inside Elections ratings supply a separate baseline when no numeric polls are loaded: favored-party chances of 99% (Solid), 90% (Likely), 75% (Lean), 60% (Tilt) and 50% (Toss-up). These are our uncalibrated assumptions, not the publisher’s probabilities. Ratings do not get combined with polling as independent evidence. A synthetic margin with six-point standard deviation supports scenario tests.

Research assumptions, not fitted parameters

Error sizes and decay rates have not been calibrated on held-out elections. The model is a reproducible research starting point. It does not yet include fitted fundamentals, fundraising, candidate quality or a full ranked-choice simulation.

How to use the challenge prices

The contest ranks final simulated capital. A high win probability alone does not imply an attractive price. Compare an independently supported probability with the executable ask and allow for depth, fees and settlement rules.

Separate party contracts can overlap in exposure. An independent victory makes “Republican NO” different from “Democratic YES.” Opposite contracts must be mapped to their exact settlement events.

Validation before reliance

Use election-cycle holdouts and as-of data. Compare Brier score, log loss, calibration curves and margin coverage with naive and market benchmarks. Audit district changes and include all races, not only competitive contests.

Until complete maps and caucus rules are verified, chamber-control forecasts are withheld. Poll-based D/R approximations also omit the chance of other-party winners.
